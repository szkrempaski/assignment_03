Honestly, the part that tripped me up the most was process_files.py, and it
wasn't the parsing. It was figuring out why my first instinct (just process
the file whenever one's uploaded) was wrong. I kept staring at it thinking
"but the file IS there, why would it recount it," until I actually traced
through what happens on a rerun: the uploader just keeps handing back the
same file over and over, forever, until someone uploads a different one. The
button was the piece I was missing. It's only True on the exact rerun where
it got clicked, and false every other time, which is what actually stops the
double-counting. Once that clicked (no pun intended) it made a lot of the
"why session state" stuff from class make more sense too, since I'd been
treating st.session_state as just a place to stash a number, not as the thing
that separates "this survives a rerun" from "this doesn't." I feel solid on
the simple counter version of this. I'd be lying if I said I'd get it right
on the first try in something more complicated, like an app where you can
edit or delete a past entry. That's the part I'd actually want to sit down
and practice next.
